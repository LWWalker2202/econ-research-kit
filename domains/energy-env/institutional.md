# Energy and Environmental Economics: Institutional Background

## Institutional background

Chinese environmental policy between the mid-2010s and the early 2020s combined three regulatory logics that are usually studied separately: campaign-style central inspection, permit-based routine supervision, and market-based pricing of emissions. The combination is what makes the period usable for empirical work. Each instrument is assigned to a different unit of observation (province, firm, sector, city), starts on a different date, and operates on a different compliance margin, so a province-year panel can carry several distinct treatment variables at once.

中央环保督察 (Central Environmental Protection Inspection, CEPI) is the campaign-style component. Inspection teams are dispatched by the Party Central Committee and the State Council, are resident in a province for roughly one month, accept public complaints, conduct on-site checks at firms, and issue a feedback report. Provinces must then submit a rectification plan and report progress, and the results enter the assessment of local officials. The first round was organized into four batches in 2016 and 2017 and covered all provincial-level regions, which gives staggered province-level entry dates. Because later rounds (look-back inspections and a new round that started in 2019) revisit earlier provinces, treated status is not absorbing, and the interpretation of a single post dummy changes across rounds.

排污许可 (pollutant discharge permit) is the routine, firm-level component. The permit system was rebuilt from 2016 onwards and permits were issued industry by industry: thermal power and pulp-and-paper firms received permits first, other industries followed in sequence. A permit fixes the allowed discharge volume and concentration for each outlet, which makes firm-level permit status, permitted limits, and compliance status observable and dated. This is the closest thing to a firm-level regulatory treatment that Chinese administrative data offer.

碳排放权交易试点 (pilot emissions trading scheme, pilot ETS) is the market-based component. Seven provinces and cities launched pilots between 2013 and 2014 with different coverage thresholds, different allowance allocations, and different price floors. A national scheme covering the power sector followed in 2021. The pilot period is therefore a staggered, subnational, price-based treatment, while the national scheme is a sector-wide boundary shift.

河长制 (river chief system) assigns named local officials to water bodies and is relevant mainly for water-quality outcomes. 能耗双控 (dual control of energy consumption) sets both a total and an intensity target for energy use and is written into the five-year plans, which yields city- and province-level target variables. 大气污染防治行动计划 (Air Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan, the "Ten Measures" of 2013) supplied the first binding PM2.5 targets for key regions and remains the reference point for pre-period pollution trends.

Why this matters for identification: the instruments differ in the level at which they bind, the date at which they bind, and the margin they constrain. That is the source of variation. It is also the source of the main threat, because several instruments overlap in the same years and their boundaries do not coincide.

## Ready-to-cite paragraphs

The following two paragraphs are written to be pasted into an Institutional Background section. They contain no figures that need citation, but the dates should be checked against the official documents listed under Sources before submission.

China's central environmental inspection system has operated in rounds since 2016. In each round, inspection teams formed by the central government are stationed in a province for about a month, where they review local enforcement records, accept complaints from residents, and conduct unannounced visits to industrial facilities. After the team leaves, the province receives a written feedback report and is required to publish a rectification plan with named responsible agencies and deadlines. Rectification progress is subsequently reviewed by the central authority and is reflected in the assessment of local officials. Because the first round was implemented in four successive batches, provinces entered the inspection cycle at different times, and the intensity of local enforcement rose sharply during and immediately after the period of stationing.

Environmental regulation in the same period was not a single instrument. Alongside inspection, the government rebuilt the pollutant discharge permit system so that each firm's allowed discharge was written into a permit and monitored outlet by outlet, replaced the pollutant discharge fee with an environmental protection tax that is set at different rates in different provinces, and introduced carbon pricing first through seven subnational pilot schemes and later through a national scheme covering the power sector. These instruments bound at different administrative levels and on different margins: inspection suspends production, permits cap discharge, and carbon pricing raises the cost of fossil fuel use. Any evaluation of one instrument therefore has to state which other instruments were binding at the same time and in the same places.
