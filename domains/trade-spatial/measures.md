# International Trade and Spatial Economics: Constructing the Treatment Variable

## Constructing the treatment variable

Trade and spatial economics rarely has a single clean treatment. What the literature calls the treatment is usually an exposure measure: a policy change or a change in accessibility that is weighted by something observed before the change. Four definitions cover most of the papers, and the choice between them determines what the estimate means.

| Definition | Data source | Implied assumption | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tariff exposure: statutory tariff at the industry or product level, weighted by pre-period trade and mapped to firms or regions by their pre-period industry mix | Customs tariff schedule; customs microdata or input-output tables for the weights | Regional industry composition before the reform is unrelated to post-reform outcome trends except through the tariff | Product- or industry-level liberalization; firm or city panels with a pre-period census |
| Import competition shock: shift-share (Bartik) measure combining pre-period local industry shares with national import growth per industry | Customs or CEPII-BACI imports; an establishment census or the industrial firms database for shares | Either the shares are exogenous, or the industry shocks are exogenous conditional on the shares | City, county, or commuting-zone panels around WTO accession |
| Market access: change in a market potential index built from travel times or transport costs and weighted by destination income | Transport network, timetable, and travel-time data; regional GDP for the weights | Route placement and network upgrading are unrelated to unobserved local growth trends | High-speed rail openings, highway expansion, port investment |
| Zone or pilot designation: indicator for a pilot free trade zone, e-commerce pilot zone, or development-zone upgrade, usually paired with an exposure mapping | Official lists of designated zones and their designation dates | Designation is unrelated to pre-existing growth conditional on controls, and spillovers stay inside the defined market | City panels with staggered designation; place-based policy evaluation |

Four practical notes. First, the exposure measure inherits the timing of its weights: fixing the weights in a pre-period year is what makes a shift-share a shift-share, and changing the base year changes the estimate, so the base year has to be argued rather than defaulted. Second, tariffs act at the product line but outcomes are usually measured at the firm or region level, and the mapping (employment share, output share, or input-output linkage) is an assumption that referees will read as a choice. Third, market access is a continuous dose, so the design needs a statement about the functional form and about what makes the travel-time change exogenous, not only a corridor-level difference-in-differences. Fourth, designation treatments are absorbing in the coding but often not in reality, because pilot zones are expanded, merger of zones occurs, and a city can receive a second designation that overlaps the first.

## Identification assumptions by definition

**Tariff exposure.** The identifying assumption is that, conditional on industry and year fixed effects and on the pre-period industry mix, the tariff change for an industry is unrelated to its counterfactual outcome trend. This fails when the products that were cut most were also those facing falling world demand. Falsification: pre-trends by exposure decile, placebo exposure built from the pre-1990s unilateral cuts, and industry-specific demand controls built from world imports. Report the first-stage relationship between the statutory tariff and the observed import price, because a tariff that is not passed through does not shift the margin that the theory describes.

**Import competition shift-share.** There are two different identification claims behind the same formula, and a paper has to say which one it is making. In the shares route, the claim is that pre-period local industry shares are unrelated to local outcome trends, so any industry-level shock is as good as random for the purpose; in the shocks route, the claim is that the industry shocks are as-if randomly assigned and the shares only weight them. The two routes imply different diagnostics: the shares route needs a test of pre-trends by share, the shocks route needs a test that the shocks are not concentrated in a few industries or driven by a single trading partner, and both need a leave-one-industry-out exercise.

**Market access.** The assumption is that the change in accessibility is not anticipated and not chosen for reasons correlated with the outcome. In practice, high-speed rail stations are placed where the corridor is cheapest to build, which is correlated with terrain and with existing population, so the exposure measure has to be conditioned on the factors that also determine the trend. The useful checks are an event study with leads, a comparison of cities whose corridor was planned before the outcome window but realized inside it, and a control for distance to the corridor line so that the estimate is identified by being near the line rather than by being large.

**Zone or pilot designation.** The assumption is selection on observables, and it is weak. Designation is decided by the central government but proposed by provincial governments that selected their strongest candidates, so the comparison group is not a random set of cities. The workable version compares designated cities with cities that were shortlisted and lost, or uses a matched sample on pre-period growth, port throughput, and foreign investment, and reports how the estimate moves when the matching changes. Spillovers are the second problem: a designation is a city-level boundary that does not contain the economic activity it attracts, so a ring of neighboring cities is part of the design rather than a robustness check.

## Common construction errors

| Error | Consequence | Correct approach |
|---|---|---|
| Using contemporaneous employment shares to weight the shock | The shares move with the shock, so the measure is endogenous by construction | Fix the weights in a pre-period year and report sensitivity to alternative base years |
| Merging HS codes across revisions without a concordance | Product-level exposure is measured at the wrong product and the panel has artificial entry and exit | Use an official or published concordance and report the share of trade left unmatched (to confirm against the official codebook) |
| Pooling processing and ordinary trade in the customs aggregate | Tariff exposure is overstated, because processing imports entered duty-free | Split by trade mode, or build the exposure measure on ordinary trade only |
| Treating a zone or rail opening as a city-level binary without a spillover ring | The control group is contaminated and the estimate picks up displacement rather than creation | Add neighboring cities and an exposure gradient, or use a travel-time design instead of a binary |
| Using straight-line distance where the mechanism is network travel time | Measurement error is systematic, because geography and the network are differently shaped | Use timetable-based travel time and report straight-line distance as a robustness check |
| Reading light intensity as economic activity without addressing saturation | The effect is attenuated in urban cores and inflated at the edges | Cross-calibrate the sensors and report results at several thresholds, or use light only as a control |
