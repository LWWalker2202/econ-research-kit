# Industrial organization and regulation: institutional background

## Institutional background

Industrial organization research on China draws its variation from four layers of regulation built at different times, run by different agencies, and binding on different margins: competition-law enforcement, administered pricing, entry regulation, and local place-based policy. The layers do not nest: in one year a firm can sit inside a development zone, be party to a notifiable merger, and sell an item still on a government price catalogue, each status changing on its own date. That is what makes the period usable for identification, and why a single policy dummy is rarely a credible treatment variable here.

反垄断法 (Anti-Monopoly Law, AML) took effect in 2008 and created merger control: mandatory pre-notification above statutory turnover thresholds, a review clock, and three published outcomes — clearance, clearance with remedies, and prohibition. Enforcement was split across three agencies until 2018, when 商务部 (Ministry of Commerce) merger review, 国家发展改革委 (National Development and Reform Commission) price-related conduct, and 国家工商行政管理总局 (State Administration for Industry and Commerce) non-price conduct were consolidated into 国家市场监督管理总局 (State Administration for Market Regulation, SAMR). Two consequences matter: case counts before and after 2018 are not comparable, because the reporting unit and case classification changed with the agency; and only notified cases are observable, so any sample of reviewed mergers is selected on the notification decision, which is not random in market size.

Administered pricing runs through two catalogues, the central 中央定价目录 and the provincial 地方定价目录. An item in a catalogue is priced by the government or held to a government band; an item that leaves it is priced by the market. Revisions of the central catalogue have moved whole product groups to market pricing, and the 2015 liberalization of drug prices removed government pricing for most drugs in a single step (to confirm against the official codebook). Because the catalogues are published with dated revisions, liberalization is observable item by item before any outcome is measured, which is unusual among Chinese treatments.

Entry regulation has two instruments. 市场准入负面清单 (the market access negative list) began as a 2013 pilot in the Shanghai free trade zone, became national in 2018, and has been revised since; each version names prohibited or restricted industries, so the restrictions covering an industry change across versions. The 2014 company-law registration reform replaced paid-in with subscribed capital and cut registration cost, and later licence consolidation reduced the approvals needed to start operating (to confirm against the official codebook). Registration records carry registration and deregistration dates, which make entry and exit measurable in the full population rather than in a size-thresholded sample.

Local policy supplies the fourth layer. 开发区 (development zones) — national economic and technological zones from 1984, high-technology zones from 1988, and many provincial zones — offer tax treatment, land, and faster approval inside a published boundary, each with a designation date. 去产能 (capacity reduction) from 2016 assigned province-by-industry targets in steel, coal, cement, and other sectors, and 环保限产 (production restriction and suspension) in autumn and winter heating seasons assigns city-by-sector output limits, most visibly in the "2+26" cities around Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei (to confirm against the official codebook). The last two are usually studied as environmental policy, but here they are shocks to market structure: they remove capacity, raise measured concentration, and move markups.

Why this matters: concentration, markups, and entry are equilibrium objects, and all four layers act on them at once, so the unmodelled layer becomes the omitted variable. The institutions also decide what the market is: industry codes are administrative constructs that change across revisions, while the antitrust market is defined by substitutability, and the gap between them biases estimates more where the industry is concentrated.

## Ready-to-cite paragraphs

Both paragraphs can be pasted into an Institutional Background section. They contain no figures, but check dates and document titles against official sources before submission.

China's merger control regime dates from the Anti-Monopoly Law of 2008, which requires parties to a concentration meeting turnover thresholds to notify the competition authority before completion. The authority reviews the notified transaction within a statutory clock and publishes one of three outcomes: clearance, clearance subject to conditions such as divestiture or behavioural commitments, and prohibition. Merger review sat in the Ministry of Commerce until 2018, when it was consolidated with price-related and non-price conduct enforcement in the State Administration for Market Regulation. Because case lists are published with dates and remedies, the timing and form of intervention are observable at the transaction level, although transactions that were never notified do not appear.

Price and entry regulation operated through published catalogues rather than case-by-case decisions. Products in the central or provincial pricing catalogue are priced by the government or constrained by a government band, and successive revisions have moved product groups into market pricing. Entry is governed by the market access negative list, reissued in successive versions, and by the company registration system simplified in 2014. Local governments designate development zones with defined boundaries that carry tax, land, and approval advantages, and during the 2016 supply-side reform and the autumn-winter heating seasons they administered capacity-reduction targets and output restrictions. These instruments were introduced on different dates and at different administrative levels, and they all move market structure, so a study of concentration, markups, or entry has to state which of them were active in the same industry and years.
