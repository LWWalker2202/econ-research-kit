# Economic history: how to walk the eight stages

## In one sentence
Economic history argues from surviving records, and survival is itself selected; so ask first who got recorded, then how the recorded quantities convert into comparable units.

## Stage 0 feasibility: three questions to ask first
- Ask how records survived. Read "Known issues" in `data.md` and establish the rule under which your archives or gazetteers survived, and whether missingness is related to the outcome.
- Ask how definitions bridge. Read "Constructing the treatment variable" in `measures.md` and confirm the mapping between historical levies, customs duties, or quotas and the modern variable you construct.
- Ask about boundaries and names. Read the third "What referees ask" item in `pitfalls.md` and confirm that historical counties can be matched to modern ones (to confirm against the official codebook).

## Stage 1 question and literature
- Read "Institutional background" in `institutional.md`, lay out the levy, registration, and treaty-port structure, and pick one institution that changed and can be measured.
- Read "Constructing the treatment variable" in `measures.md` and choose among port opening, county fiscal quota, rationing intensity, and the market-over-plan price ratio.
- Check that the modern reconstruction of historical statistics in the paper you follow matches yours, especially the price deflation.

## Stage 2 identification
- For port opening, read "Identification assumptions by definition" in `measures.md` and discuss how the ports were chosen relative to their hinterlands.
- For a fiscal quota, show that the quota was set without reference to the outcome you study.
- For a dual-price ratio, show that the scope of market and plan prices can be delimited from the historical sources.

## Stage 3 data
- Read "Data sources" in `data.md` and separate what customs returns, gazetteers, newspapers, firm archives, historical boundaries, and republican yearbooks each contribute.
- Read "Variables and definitions": fix the original units of trade values, grain prices, land-tax quotas, and population, and the deflation method.
- Read "Known issues" and register the mechanism of missing records, unit conversion, and place-name change.

## Stage 4 estimation
- Use "Common construction errors" in `measures.md` to check that nominal magnitudes are not compared across periods without deflation.
- Handle selection openly: estimate on the counties with surviving records and on all counties, then report the difference.
- Report results under alternative deflators and alternative boundary matches.

## Stage 5 tables and figures
- Build the balance table around the first referee question in `pitfalls.md`: whether record survival correlates with county characteristics.
- The time-series figure should mark institutional changes and changes in the unit of measurement.
- State original units and conversion factors in the main table (to confirm against the official codebook).

## Stage 6 report
- Report the sources and the survival mechanism, following the first "How to respond" item in `pitfalls.md`.
- Report how boundary matching was done and what share of observations failed to match.
- Report the full deflation and conversion procedure.

## Stage 7 writing
- Start the institutional section from the "Ready-to-cite paragraphs" in `institutional.md`, and get the names of the archives and gazetteers right.
- Cite archives, gazetteers, and yearbooks only; leave page and volume numbers for (to confirm against the official codebook).

## Stage 8 referee response
- Prepare the "What referees ask" items in `pitfalls.md`; selection, cross-period deflation, and county matching are the usual three.
- When the coding rule is contested, answer with the "publish the rule and a spot-check" response in "How to respond".

## The three things most often missed
- Treating counties with surviving records as a random sample.
- Deflating historical magnitudes with a modern price index without saying so.
- Ignoring the sample lost when historical and modern boundaries do not line up.
