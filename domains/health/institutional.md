# Health Economics: Institutional Background

## Institutional background

Chinese health insurance is not one scheme. It is three schemes with different enrollee populations, different financing rules, and different benefit packages, and the reforms that changed them are the main source of identifying variation in the field. A paper set in China usually identifies off a reform that changed who was covered, at what rate they were reimbursed, or what a hospital was allowed to charge, and the credibility of that paper depends on describing the scheme structure correctly.

城镇职工基本医疗保险 (Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance, UEBMI) covers formal-sector employees and was established at the city level in the late 1990s. 新型农村合作医疗 (New Cooperative Medical Scheme, NCMS) was piloted in 2003 and expanded to rural counties through 2008, with voluntary household enrollment, county-level risk pools, and reimbursement schedules that varied by county and by level of care. 城镇居民基本医疗保险 (Urban Resident Basic Medical Insurance, URBMI) followed in 2007 for urban residents outside formal employment, which included children, students, and the elderly not covered by UEBMI. The two resident schemes began to be merged into one 城乡居民基本医疗保险 (Resident Basic Medical Insurance, RBMI) from 2016 onward, province by province.

On top of the basic schemes sits supplementary coverage. 大病保险 (critical illness insurance) was introduced from 2012 to reimburse high out-of-pocket spending above a threshold, financed from the basic scheme funds and administered by commercial insurers. Its rollout dates and thresholds differ by locality, which makes it a usable staggered treatment for financial protection outcomes.

The supply side changed at the same time. The 2009 reform package introduced the 基本药物制度 (essential medicines system) and zero-markup sales at primary care facilities. 公立医院改革 (public hospital reform) extended the zero-markup policy on drugs to public hospitals, with a nationwide push around 2017, and replaced drug revenue with service fees and fiscal subsidies. 分级诊疗 (hierarchical diagnosis and treatment) and 医联体 (medical alliances) tried to shift visits toward primary care. From 2018, 药品集中采购 (centralized volume-based procurement) cut prices of selected drugs through national tenders, and 国家医保局 (National Healthcare Security Administration) began negotiating prices for the reimbursement list. Payment reform toward DRG and DIP prospective payment started as local pilots.

Why these institutions matter for identification: eligibility is set by hukou status and by employment category, benefit generosity is set at the county or city level, and the reforms arrive on different dates in different places. The variation is real, but the schemes overlap, so a claim about one scheme must show that the others did not change at the same time for the same people. Where the outcome is a health effect of pollution or of the environment rather than of insurance, the measurement and identification issues are covered in `domains/energy-env`; this file covers only the exposure-to-health path.

## Ready-to-cite paragraphs

China's health insurance system consists of three schemes that differ in who they cover and how generously they reimburse. Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance covers formal-sector employees and is administered at the city level. The New Cooperative Medical Scheme, piloted in rural counties from 2003 and expanded through 2008, covered rural residents through voluntary household enrollment in county-level risk pools. Urban Resident Basic Medical Insurance, introduced in 2007, extended coverage to urban residents outside formal employment. Because enrollment rules, reimbursement schedules, and pool administration are all set locally, the effective benefit package differs across counties and over time, which is the institutional basis for the variation used in this paper [to fill]. From 2016, the two resident schemes were merged into a single Resident Basic Medical Insurance, province by province [verify].

Two further layers shape financial protection. Critical illness insurance, introduced from 2012, reimburses out-of-pocket spending above a local threshold and is financed from basic scheme funds, so it targets the upper tail of the spending distribution rather than the average enrollee. On the supply side, the 2009 reform introduced the essential medicines system and zero-markup drug sales at primary care facilities, public hospital reform extended zero markup on drugs to hospitals around 2017, and centralized volume-based procurement from 2018 reduced prices for selected drugs through national tenders. These supply-side changes alter what a visit costs and what is prescribed, so they enter as separate treatments rather than as parts of the insurance expansion.
