# Theory and mechanism design: institutional background

A theory paper's institutional section is not policy history. It justifies the assumption list: who holds which information, who moves first, who can commit, and which of those claims are facts about Chinese institutions rather than modelling convenience. Referees look for the assumption the paper cannot do without, and for evidence that the institution quoted in its defence obliges. This file records those institutions, the dates that make them plausible, and two paragraphs adaptable into a Model Environment section.

## Institutional background

**Information: who knows what.** The fiscal and administrative hierarchy places the central government at the top of a reporting chain while local governments hold the finer information about firms and households in their jurisdictions. Environmental monitoring was long produced locally and reported upward, and recentralising the authority in the 2010s was justified by the tension between local information and central verification (to confirm against the official codebook). Statistics follow the same shape: firm and household data are collected by local offices, checked against administrative targets, and aggregated upward under political assessment. In finance, banks accumulate soft information through repeated dealings, formal credit registries cover a narrower set of obligations, and listed firms disclose far more than unlisted ones. The implication is concrete: a screening or moral-hazard assumption must name the agent that lacks the signal, the institution that fails to transmit it, and why the gap survives the incentive to report.

**Timing: who moves first.** The central-local relationship is the workhorse leader-follower setting. The centre sets tax-sharing rules, land quotas, environmental targets, and transfers before local governments choose implementation intensity, and the planning cycle gives those announcements a horizon longer than one budget year. Local governments act as leaders toward firms in industrial policy, land supply, and infrastructure. In platform and financial regulation the observed order is often the reverse, with rules arriving after the market has scaled, which is why papers assuming commitment before entry and papers assuming reaction after entry reach different conclusions. A timing assumption is defensible when the paper names the instrument, its holder, and the moment it is set.

**Commitment: which promises are credible.** Property and contract rights are protected by law and enforced through courts, while policy continuity is bounded by the finite horizon of local officials and by discretion retained over industrial policy, land supply, credit allocation, and property-market rules. Some commitments are credible because they are delegated to rules or agencies, some because reversal would be costly, some not at all, and papers assuming the last kind should treat them as an upper bound. Implicit guarantees, for instance the expectation that local financing vehicles will be supported, are commitments created by belief rather than statute, and models relying on them must explain what sustains the belief.

**Property rights and factor allocation: the simplification zone.** Urban land is state-owned, rural land collectively owned, land supply is administered locally, state firms carry policy burdens and softer budget constraints, capital allocation is shaped by ownership and lending quotas, labour is segmented by household registration, and several energy prices remain administered. These are the places where complete markets, price-taking, and a representative household are simplifications rather than descriptions. The paper should say so and say what the simplification buys: tractability, a closed-form comparative static, or isolation of one mechanism.

## Ready-to-cite paragraphs

China's fiscal and administrative structure places the central government at the top of a hierarchy in which local governments hold much of the information about firms and households. Firm and household statistics are collected locally, checked against administrative targets, and aggregated upward under political assessment, and environmental monitoring followed the same pattern until its authority was recentralised (to confirm against the official codebook). In financial markets, banks accumulate soft information through repeated dealings, formal credit registries cover a narrower set of obligations, and listed firms disclose far more than unlisted ones. A model in which the upper level holds a coarse signal and the lower level a fine one therefore describes the reporting relationship rather than an arbitrary information technology. The same structure fixes timing: the centre announces tax-sharing rules, land quotas, environmental targets, and transfers before local governments choose implementation intensity, and the planning cycle lengthens the horizon of those announcements.
